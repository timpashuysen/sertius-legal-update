Background to the dispute 20 The background to the dispute, as set out in paragraphs 2 to 35 of the judgment under appeal, may be summarised as follows for the purposes of the present proceedings. 21 Following an inquiry into the detailed rules for the implementation of the cross-compliance system, provided for in Title VI of Regulation No 1306/2013, in the Autonomous Community of Castilla y León (Spain), which the Commission initiated in the exercise of its conformity clearance power under, inter alia, Article 52 of that regulation, that institution found several deficiencies in respect of three key controls and one ancillary control for claim year 2016 et seq. 22 According to the Commission, those deficiencies were liable to cause a risk to the Funds and, therefore, lead to the application of a flat-rate financial correction on the total amount of aid paid to the beneficiaries to whom the cross-compliance system applied. 23 In that regard, the Commission addressed several recommendations to the Spanish authorities, including, in particular, the taking of immediate corrective measures in order to implement appropriate checks of cross-compliance, in accordance with Article 96(3) and Article 97(1) of Regulation No 1306/2013, and Article 24(1) and Article 65(1) of Implementing Regulation No 809/2014 (‘the cross-compliance checks’). 24 Following exchanges between the Spanish authorities and the Commission, and the report of the conciliation body, that institution forwarded to the Spanish authorities its final conclusions (‘the final position’), in which it confirmed that the deficiencies found in the first and second key controls had created a risk for the Funds. In all other respects, the Commission closed the inspection. 25 In this first key control, which concerned the ‘nature and extent of [on-the-spot cross-compliance] checks’ in respect of the SMR, the Commission found four infringements. The first infringement concerned the lack of evidence that cross-compliance checks had been carried out for SMR 1 on the protection of waters against pollution caused by nitrates from agricultural sources. The second infringement concerned SMR 3 and related to the lack of cross-compliance checks for SMR 3 in relation to the conservation of the natural habitats of wild flora in claim years 2016 and 2017. In particular, the Commission found that, in the Autonomous Community of Castilla y León, the Spanish authorities had not carried out cross-compliance checks in order to verify effective compliance with the protection of the protected species of wild flora listed in Annex II to Directive 92/43, in accordance with Article 6(1) and (2) of that directive. The third and fourth infringements concerned the insufficient quality of the cross-compliance checks of SMR 7 and SMR 8 carried out by the Spanish authorities on the identification and registration of bovine animals (SMR 7), and ovine and caprine animals (SMR 8) in claim years 2016 and 2017 and, despite the corrective measures adopted, also for claim year 2018. 26 In view of the deficiencies found in the final position, the Commission concluded that, for claim years 2016 and 2017, a flat-rate financial correction of 5% of 10% of the total amount of aid and, for claim year 2018, a flat-rate financial correction of 2% of 10% of the total amount of aid, should be applied. 27 For those reasons, the Commission, by the decision at issue, excluded from EU financing the total gross amount of EUR 9 973 274.36, which, in view of the amounts already recovered following the investigations prior to the one referred to in paragraph 21 above, resulted in a net financial correction of EUR 9 968 215.15. The procedure before the General Court and the judgment under appeal 28 By application of 19 August 2022, the Kingdom of Spain brought an action before the General Court seeking the annulment of the decision at issue in so far as it concerned that Member State. In support of its action, it raised two pleas in law, based on various errors of fact, law and assessment allegedly committed by the Commission. 29 The first plea in law contained six parts, the first to fourth of which concerned the lack of evidence of serious and reasonable doubt regarding the various deficiencies established in respect of the first and second key controls. The fifth part of that plea alleged a breach of the principle of good administration vitiating the rejection of the calculation of the amount to be excluded from EU financing based on an extrapolation proposed by the Kingdom of Spain. The sixth part of that plea and the second plea alleged a breach of the principle of proportionality vitiating the flat-rate financial correction imposed by the Commission. 30 By the judgment under appeal, the General Court upheld, in its entirety, the second part of the first plea and, in part, the fourth part of that plea, on the ground that the Commission had incorrectly based the flat-rate financial correction at issue, first, on the failure, attributed to the Kingdom of Spain, to carry out the cross-compliance checks of SMR 3 and, second, on the failure consisting in an infringement of Article 72(1)(b)(ii) of Implementing Regulation No 809/2014, in so far as the reports of the cross-compliance checks did not include the signature of the beneficiaries. By that judgment, the General Court rejected the first and third parts of the first plea. It did not examine the fifth and sixth parts of the first plea or the second plea. 31 As regards the second part of the first plea, first of all, the General Court noted, in paragraphs 71 and 72 of the judgment under appeal, that, under Article 93(1) of Regulation No 1306/2013, the SMR laid down in Annex II to that regulation constitute the rules on cross-compliance and that, for SMR 3, Annex II refers to Article 6(1) and (2) of Directive 92/43. The General Court also found, in paragraph 73 of that judgment, that, in Castilla y León, Article 6(1) and (2) of Directive 92/43 was transposed by Decree 63/2007, which contains a list of the protected species of wild flora in the Autonomous Community of Castilla y León. 32 Next, in paragraph 76 of that judgment, the General Court found that the combination, on the one hand, of Decree 63/2007, which recognises the presence of protected species of wild flora in Castilla y León, and on the other, of the lack of cross-compliance checks in respect of SMR 3, aimed specifically at protecting such species, constitutes evidence of serious and reasonable doubt as to the checks carried out. It inferred from this, in paragraph 77 of that judgment, that it was for the Kingdom of Spain to prove that the Commission’s findings were incorrect. 33 Lastly, the General Court emphasised, in paragraph 78 of the judgment under appeal, that the obligation to carry out cross-compliance checks of SMR 3 relates only to compliance with the measures transposing Directive 92/43 at national level, given that, according to Article 93(2) of Regulation No 1306/2013, the legal acts referred to in Annex II to that regulation, relating to the SMR, apply in the version in force and, in the case of directives, in the version implemented by the Member States. However, in paragraph 79 of that judgment, the General Court held that, in the present case, in the absence of any conservation measure previously defined by the Spanish authorities within the meaning of Article 6(1) and (2) of that directive, the requirements imposed on farmers in respect of SMR 3 were not identified in sufficiently precise terms for compliance with those requirements to be checked effectively by those authorities. 34 In those circumstances, in the last sentence of paragraph 79 and in paragraph 82 of that judgment, the General Court held, respectively, first, that the arguments of the Kingdom of Spain had demonstrated that the absence of cross-compliance checks of SMR 3 could not be regarded by the Commission as non-compliance and, second, that that Member State had refuted the Commission’s conclusion in the final position that the cross-compliance checks of SMR 3 were not compliant. 35 By the judgment under appeal, the General Court annulled the decision at issue in its entirety in so far as it concerned the Kingdom of Spain. 36 As regards the extent of that annulment, the General Court stated, in essence, that, were it not for the deficiencies referred to in the second and fourth parts of the first plea in law, the calculation of the total amount of the flat-rate financial correction would normally have been different and that it is therefore for the Commission to draw the appropriate conclusions from that annulment. The appeal 39 The present appeal proceedings concern only the second of the four infringements found in the context of the first key control, which are referred to in paragraph 25 above. 40 In support of its appeal, the Commission raises two grounds, alleging that the General Court erred in law, first, in the application of the rules governing the allocation of the burden of proof and, second, in the interpretation of the obligations incumbent on the Member States as regards monitoring compliance with the SMR, in accordance with Regulation No 1306/2013. Arguments of the parties 41 By its second ground of appeal, which it is appropriate to examine first, the Commission submits that, in paragraphs 78 to 80 and 82 of the judgment under appeal, the General Court erred in law. In particular, it incorrectly held that the Commission’s conclusion set out in the final position, according to which, in the Autonomous Community of Castilla y León, the cross-compliance checks of SMR 3 were not compliant, had been refuted by the Kingdom of Spain on the ground that it had relied on the lack of a definition of any conservation measure, within the meaning of Article 6(1) and (2) of Directive 92/43, in the Spanish law applicable to that autonomous community. The General Court therefore concluded that, in those circumstances, it was for the Kingdom of Spain to prove that the Commission’s findings were incorrect. However, in order to be able to invalidate those findings, the Kingdom of Spain should have demonstrated the absence of protected species of wild flora on agricultural parcels or pasture in the Autonomous Community of Castilla y León; merely calling into question the Commission’s on-the-spot visits was not sufficient for that purpose. 82 By the second of those arguments, the Kingdom of Spain alleged that there were no agricultural parcels or pasture in that autonomous community situated in special areas of conservation for the habitats of protected species of wild flora. Suffice it to note that that argument, in so far as it constitutes an offer of evidence submitted for the first time at the hearing at first instance, without justification for the delay in its submission, must be regarded as being out of time and, therefore, inadmissible. 83 In those circumstances, the second part of the first plea in law put forward by the Kingdom of Spain at first instance must be rejected. 84 However, the state of proceedings does not permit final judgment.
