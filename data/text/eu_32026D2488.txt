L_202602488EN.000101.fmx.xml Official Journal of the European Union EN L series 2026/2488 11.11.2026 COMMISSION IMPLEMENTING DECISION (EU) 2026/2488 of 10 November 2026 laying down rules for the application of Directive (EU) 2024/2881 of the European Parliament and of the Council as regards technical details for demonstration and subtraction of exceedances attributable to natural sources (Text with EEA relevance) THE EUROPEAN COMMISSION, Having regard to the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, Having regard to Directive (EU) 2024/2881 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 23 October 2024 on ambient air quality and cleaner air for Europe ( 1 ) , and in particular Article 16(4) thereof, Whereas: (1) Directive (EU) 2024/2881 establishes air quality standards and air quality monitoring and reporting obligations to ensure a high level of protection of human health and the environment. (2) Article 16 of Directive (EU) 2024/2881 allows Member States to identify zones and average exposure territorial units where exceedances of certain air quality standards are attributable to natural sources. Member States are to provide the Commission with lists of any such zones and average exposure territorial units, together with information on concentrations and sources and the evidence demonstrating that the exceedances are attributable to natural sources. When the Commission has been informed accordingly, such exceedances are not to be considered as an exceedance for the purpose of Directive (EU) 2024/2881. (3) Although Directive (EU) 2024/2881 provides for the possibility to subtract exceedances from natural sources for compliance assessment purposes, the health risks associated with high pollution concentrations, even where they originate from natural sources, remain. HAS ADOPTED THIS DECISION: Article 1 Principles for the identification, demonstration, quantification and subtraction of contributions from natural sources For the purposes of demonstrating that exceedances are attributable to natural sources in accordance with Article 16(1) and (2) of Directive (EU) 2024/2881, Member States shall comply with the following principles: (a) provide robust evidence demonstrating that contributions leading to exceedances are attributable to natural sources and were not caused directly or indirectly by human activities, by providing the required information in Article 5; (b) ensure that the occurrence of the specific natural event is demonstrated using appropriate methods; (c) ensure that the quantification of the contributions from natural sources is sufficiently precise and accurate; (d) ensure that the quantification of the contributions from natural sources is temporally consistent with the averaging periods of the air quality standard considered; (e) ensure that the quantification of the contributions from natural sources is spatially consistent with the natural event; (f) ensure that the contributions from natural sources are quantified for each pollutant separately; (g) ensure that the method to determine the contributions from natural sources is sufficiently documented in accordance with Article 5. Article 2 Identification and quantification of contributions from natural sources 1. Member States may apply any appropriate methods and methodologies for the identification and quantification of the contributions from natural sources provided that the principles set out in Article 1 are complied with. 2. Member States shall perform a qualitative assessment to identify and determine the occurrence, timing, duration and spatial extent of the contributions from natural sources. The qualitative assessment shall be performed by using a combination of methods which may include air quality measurements data, modelling applications, remote sensing observations, meteorological analyses and backward-trajectory calculations, as relevant. In the case of transport of natural particles from dry regions, Member States may support the qualitative assessment with measurement data showing a sharp increase in background concentrations of particulate matter and a relatively low PM 2,5 /PM 10 ratio. For emissions from volcanic eruptions, the qualitative assessment may be supported by data showing a simultaneous increase in particulate matter (PM 10 and PM 2,5 ) and SO 2 . 3. Member States shall quantify the daily contribution of the identified natural source to the ambient pollutant concentration expressed in the mass concentration unit relevant to the pollutant, such as μg/m 3 , in accordance with the requirements set out in Article 4. Member States may quantify the contributions from natural sources at a shorter time interval relevant to the applicable air quality standard. 4. Where the methodology used requires the use of one or several sampling points at rural background locations, those sampling points shall be located in the area affected by the contributions from natural sources that have been identified and quantified for the zone or average exposure territorial unit in which the subtractions are to be applied in accordance with Article 3. Where the methodology requires the use of sampling points but no relevant sampling point at rural background location is available, the nearest most suitable sampling point at urban background or suburban background locations shall be used. In this case, if relevant, Member States shall correct the quantification to remove any anthropogenic contributions at urban or suburban background locations. 5. Where applicable, the determination of sea salt contributions through chemical analysis shall be done for each specific sampling point affected and shall cover at least 120 sampling days per year randomly distributed throughout the year. Once the chemical analysis is performed, Member States may use modelling applications provided the chemical analysis is repeated at least every five years. The results of the chemical analysis shall apply only to that sampling point and its spatial representativeness area, unless a wider application of the results is further supported by modelling applications and additional particulate matter composition measurements at other relevant locations. 6. Member States shall consider the periodicity of the natural event, and whether it is an occasional short-term natural event or a continuous natural event with different intensities, as is the case for sea salt contributions, to determine the appropriate periodicity of measurements. 7. Member States may identify contributions from natural sources originating inside or outside their territory. Article 3 Subtraction of contributions from natural sources 1. Where a Member State decides to subtract contributions from natural sources, they shall do so by subtracting the contributions quantified in accordance with Article 2(3) from the daily concentrations at each affected sampling point for each affected day. They shall not perform such subtractions from daily concentrations for days not affected by the contributions from natural sources. When calculating an annual mean, Member States shall use the daily concentration after subtraction of contributions from natural sources. When calculating time intervals shorter than an annual mean, Member States may use a temporal resolution higher than daily concentrations. 2. When assessing compliance with the average exposure reduction obligation in accordance with Article 13(3) of Directive (EU) 2024/2881, Member States shall ensure consistency, as regards subtractions of contributions from natural sources, between the calculation of the AEI for the base year and the AEI for the target year. Where data are not available for the years included in the calculation of the AEI for the base year to enable subtraction of contributions from natural sources, Member States may use an estimated value. The estimation may be based on measured contributions from natural sources for the same zone or average exposure territorial unit in meteorologically similar years. Where subtractions are applied for a target year and that year subsequently becomes a base year, the same subtractions as for that target year shall be applied in the calculation of the new AEI for that new base year. Where subtractions are not applied for the target year, subtractions shall not be applied in the calculation of the new AEI for the new base year. 3. When considering contributions of natural sources to the AEI, contributions of natural sources shall be subtracted for each sampling point affected in accordance with the requirements of this decision. Article 4 Minimum data quality objectives and quality control 1. Data obtained for the quantification of contributions from natural sources in accordance with Article 2(3) of this Decision through the use of measurements or modelling shall meet the relevant data quality objectives for air quality assessment laid down in Annex V to Directive (EU) 2024/2881. 2. When using modelling applications to identify natural sources events, Member States shall ensure that those modelling applications are fit-for-purpose and quality assured. 3. When using modelling applications to identify the transport of natural particles from dry regions, Member States shall establish model specific threshold for when a day can be classified as influenced by contributions from natural sources. Article 5 Documentation 1. Member States shall provide the information listed in the Annex to this Decision for the purpose of fulfilling the information requirements laid down in Article 16(2) of Directive (EU) 2024/2881. 2. The documentation shall be specific to the year in which subtractions of contributions from natural sources have been performed. Article 6 Entry into force This Decision shall enter into force on the twentieth day following that of its publication in the Official Journal of the European Union .
