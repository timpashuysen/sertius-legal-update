TITLE: Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2026/2488 of 10 November 2026 laying down rules for the application of Directive (EU) 2024/2881 of the European Parliament and of the Council as regards technical details for demonstration and subtraction of exceedances attributable to natural sources

L_202602488EN.000101.fmx.xml Official Journal of the European Union EN L series 2026/2488 11.11.2026 COMMISSION IMPLEMENTING DECISION (EU) 2026/2488 of 10 November 2026 laying down rules for the application of Directive (EU) 2024/2881 of the European Parliament and of the Council as regards technical details for demonstration and subtraction of exceedances attributable to natural sources (Text with EEA relevance) THE EUROPEAN COMMISSION, Having regard to the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, Having regard to Directive (EU) 2024/2881 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 23 October 2024 on ambient air quality and cleaner air for Europe ( 1 ) , and in particular Article 16(4) thereof, Whereas: (1) Directive (EU) 2024/2881 establishes air quality standards and air quality monitoring and reporting obligations to ensure a high level of protection of human health and the environment. (2) Article 16 of Directive (EU) 2024/2881 allows Member States to identify zones and average exposure territorial units where exceedances of certain air quality standards are attributable to natural sources. Member States are to provide the Commission with lists of any such zones and average exposure territorial units, together with information on concentrations and sources and the evidence demonstrating that the exceedances are attributable to natural sources. When the Commission has been informed accordingly, such exceedances are not to be considered as an exceedance for the purpose of Directive (EU) 2024/2881. (3) Although Directive (EU) 2024/2881 provides for the possibility to subtract exceedances from natural sources for compliance assessment purposes, the health risks associated with high pollution concentrations, even where they originate from natural sources, remain. The subtraction of those contributions is not to prevent Member States from taking action to reduce their health impact, and where the applicable alert thresholds, referred to in Article 15 of Directive (EU) 2024/2881, are exceeded implement the short-term action plans required in accordance with Article 20 of Directive (EU) 2024/2881. (4) In accordance with Directive (EU) 2024/2881, the Commission is to provide technical details on the demonstration and subtraction of exceedances attributable to natural sources and to specify the content of the evidence to be provided by Member States demonstrating such exceedances. In order to ensure comparability across Member States those technical details should establish how to determine that a contribution from a natural source may be subtracted from the air pollutants measured and should set out the procedure to follow for the subtraction. Article 4, point (40), of Directive (EU) 2024/2881 provides an indicative list of natural events that are to be considered contributions from natural sources. (5) For the purpose of applying Article 16 of Directive (EU) 2024/2881 different events such as, volcanic eruptions, seismic and geothermal activity, wild land fires, sea sprays and the atmospheric resuspension or transport of natural particles from dry regions, may be considered natural sources. It is therefore appropriate to establish certain principles to guide Member States in identifying and demonstrating which contributions are attributable to natural sources and to avoid that events such as local dust re-suspension from hard surfaces in urban or rural areas, where this is by large associated with human activities and caused by, for example, traffic, road surface abrasion, and tyre and brake wear are unduly considered natural sources for the purpose of Article 16 of that Directive. (6) Different methodologies exist to demonstrate and subtract natural sources contributions ( 2 ) and are expected to evolve in the future. It is therefore appropriate to provide technical details regarding the identification and quantification of contribution from natural sources regardless of the methods and methodologies used provided that they comply with certain principles ensuring their robustness. (7) The measures provided for in this Decision are in accordance with the opinion of the Ambient Air Quality Committee, HAS ADOPTED THIS DECISION: Article 1 Principles for the identification, demonstration, quantification and subtraction of contributions from natural sources For the purposes of demonstrating that exceedances are attributable to natural sources in accordance with Article 16(1) and (2) of Directive (EU) 2024/2881, Member States shall comply with the following principles: (a) provide robust evidence demonstrating that contributions leading to exceedances are attributable to natural sources and were not caused directly or indirectly by human activities, by providing the required information in Article 5; (b) ensure that the occurrence of the specific natural event is demonstrated using appropriate methods; (c) ensure that the quantification of the contributions from natural sources is sufficiently precise and accurate; (d) ensure that the quantification of the contributions from natural sources is temporally consistent with the averaging periods of the air quality standard considered; (e) ensure that the quantification of the contributions from natural sources is spatially consistent with the natural event; (f) ensure that the contributions from natural sources are quantified for each pollutant separately; (g) ensure that the method to determine the contributions from natural sources is sufficiently documented in accordance with Article 5. Article 2 Identification and quantification of contributions from natural sources 1. Member States may apply any appropriate methods and methodologies for the identification and quantification of the contributions from natural sources provided that the principles set out in Article 1 are complied with. 2. Member States shall perform a qualitative assessment to identify and determine the occurrence, timing, duration and spatial extent of the contributions from natural sources. The qualitative assessment shall be performed by using a combination of methods which may include air quality measurements data, modelling applications, remote sensing observations, meteorological analyses and backward-trajectory calculations, as relevant. In the case of transport of natural particles from dry regions, Member States may support the qualitative assessment with measurement data showing a sharp increase in background concentrations of particulate matter and a relatively low PM 2,5 /PM 10 ratio. For emissions from volcanic eruptions, the qualitative assessment may be supported by data showing a simultaneous increase in particulate matter (PM 10 and PM 2,5 ) and SO 2 . 3. Member States shall quantify the daily contribution of the identified natural source to the ambient pollutant concentration expressed in the mass concentration unit relevant to the pollutant, such as μg/m 3 , in accordance with the requirements set out in Article 4. Member States may quantify the contributions from natural sources at a shorter time interval relevant to the applicable air quality standard. 4. Where the methodology used requires the use of one or several sampling points at rural background locations, those sampling points shall be located in the area affected by the contributions from natural sources that have been identified and quantified for the zone or average exposure territorial unit in which the subtractions are to be applied in accordance with Article 3. Where the methodology requires the use of sampling points but no relevant sampling point at rural background location is available, the nearest most suitable sampling point at urban background or suburban background locations shall be used. In this case, if relevant, Member States shall correct the quantification to remove any anthropogenic contributions at urban or suburban background locations. 5. Where applicable, the determination of sea salt contributions through chemical analysis shall be done for each specific sampling point affected and shall cover at least 120 sampling days per year randomly distributed throughout the year. Once the chemical analysis is performed, Member States may use modelling applications provided the chemical analysis is repeated at least every five years. The results of the chemical analysis shall apply only to that sampling point and its spatial representativeness area, unless a wider application of the results is further supported by modelling applications and additional particulate matter composition measurements at other relevant locations. 6. Member States shall consider the periodicity of the natural event, and whether it is an occasional short-term natural event or a continuous natural event with different intensities, as is the case for sea salt contributions, to determine the appropriate periodicity of measurements. 7.
