HAS ADOPTED THIS DECISION: Article 1 The uniform conditions for operating rules as referred to in Article 70i of Directive 2010/75/EU are those set out in the Annex to this Decision. Article 2 1. Other techniques than those listed in the Annex to this Decision may be used. In such cases, Member States shall validate, based on scientifically established methods, that the use of such techniques, as defined in Article 3, point (10)(a), of Directive 2010/75/EU ensures at least an equivalent level of environmental protection to those listed in the Annex, including human health and climate protection, also taking into account animal welfare systems recognised at Union, national or international level. 2. Environmental performances of techniques listed in the Annex to this Decision that are different from those listed in Sections 4 and 5 of that Annex may be used. In such cases, Member States shall validate such environmental performances, based on scientifically established methods. 3. When techniques listed in the Annex to this Decision are used for which the environmental performance is not specified in Sections 4 and 5 of that Annex, Member States shall validate their environmental performance. 4. When combinations of techniques are used, Member States shall validate the environmental performance of such combinations of techniques. 5. Member States shall ensure that all underlying data required by Directive 2003/4/EC of the European Parliament and of the Council ( 8 ) , methodologies, and assumptions used for the validations carried out in accordance with paragraphs 1 to 4 of this Article, are fully documented and publicly available. Article 3 1. Member States may decide to use, as an alternative to the maximum values of ammonia emissions to air set out in the Annex to this Decision, a relative system based on minimum ammonia reduction requirements at each animal house as compared to a reference system based on Union or international sources. In this case Member States shall ensure that such system provides at least the same reduction of ammonia emissions as the one achieved when using the maximum values of ammonia emissions to air set out in that Annex, and that it is based on scientifically established methods. 2. Member States referred to in paragraph 1 shall ensure that all underlying data, required by Directive 2003/4/EC, methodologies, and assumptions are fully documented and publicly available via the internet. They shall inform the Commission about the use of the alternative approach and about the underlying data, methodologies and assumptions. 3. Member States that decide, as an alternative to the maximum values of ammonia emissions to air at each animal house, to apply maximum values of ammonia emissions to air across all animal houses at the same farm, shall ensure that there is no net increase in ammonia emissions from housing in the farm. Article 4 This Decision shall apply as follows: (a) not later than 4 years from 8 October 2026 for installations covered by Annex Ia of Directive 2010/75/EU with a capacity of 600 LSU or more; (b) not later than 5 years from 8 October 2026 for installations covered by Annex Ia of Directive 2010/75/EU with a capacity of 400 LSU or more; (c) not later than 6 years from 8 October 2026 for all other installations covered by Annex Ia of Directive 2010/75/EU. Article 5 This Decision is addressed to the Member States. Done at Brussels, 8 October 2026. ANNEX UNIFORM CONDITIONS FOR OPERATING RULES FOR LIVESTOCK (PIGS AND POULTRY REARING) SCOPE These Uniform Conditions (UC) concern the activities specified in Annex Ia to Directive 2010/75/EU (the IED). The Livestock Unit (LSU) level of an installation is to be calculated using the conversion rates given in Annex Ia to the IED. In particular, the UC cover the following on-farm processes and activities: (a) nutritional management; (b) feed preparation; (c) housing; (d) manure management, including collection, storage, processing and landspreading; (e) storage of dead animals. The UC apply in parallel to other relevant legislation, e.g. Council Directive 91/676/EEC ( 1 ) , Directive (EU) 2016/2284 of the European Parliament and of the Council ( 2 ) , Regulation (EC) No 1069/2009 of the European Parliament and of the Council ( 3 ) , legislation on animal welfare (e.g. Council Directive 2008/120/EC ( 4 ) , Council Directive 98/58/EC ( 5 ) , Council Directive 1999/74/EC ( 6 ) , Council Directive 2007/43/EC ( 7 ) ). DEFINITIONS For the purposes of this Annex, the following definitions apply: General terms Term used Definition Ad libitum The provision of free access to feed or water thereby allowing the animal to self-regulate feed or water intake according to its biological needs. Animal house A plant on a farm specifically designed to house livestock. Animal place Space provided per animal in a housing system taking into account the maximum capacity of the plant. Conservation tillage Any method of soil cultivation that leaves the previous year's crop residue (such as corn stalks or wheat stubble) on the surface of fields before and after planting the next crop, to reduce soil erosion and run-off. Earth-banked slurry storage Storage of slurry in in-ground structures (e.g. lagoons, basins) constructed within compacted earth banks. Existing farm A farm which is not a new farm. Existing plant A plant which is not a new plant. Farm An installation as defined in Article 3 point (3) of the IED, where pigs or poultry are reared. Loose housing systems for farrowing sows Housing systems for farrowing sows providing larger areas for movement compared to fixed crate pens, to ensure enhanced animal welfare standards, including sufficient space to allow the sow to turn around and express natural behaviours with minimum space levels per sow. Manure Slurry and/or solid manure. Newly covered farm An existing farm carrying out activities referred to in Annex Ia and which did not fall in the scope of the IED before 4 August 2024. New farm A farm whose construction has been authorised or registered after the applicable deadline set out in Article 3(5) of Directive (EU) 2024/1785 or a complete replacement of a farm after the applicable deadline set out in Article 3(5) of that Directive. New plant A plant whose construction at the site of the farm has been authorised or registered after the applicable deadline set out in Article 3(5) of Directive (EU) 2024/1785 or a complete replacement of a plant on the existing foundations, after the applicable deadline set out in Article 3(5) of that Directive. On-farm Within the premises of the IED installation. Organic production Organic production defined in accordance with Regulation (EU) 2018/848 Plant A part of the farm where one of the following processes or activities is carried out: animal housing, manure storage, manure processing. A plant consists of a single building (or facility) and/or the necessary equipment to carry out processes or activities. Sensitive receptor Areas which need special protection, such as: (a) residential areas; (b) areas where human activities are carried out (e.g. neighbouring workplaces, schools, day-care centres, recreational areas, hospitals or nursing homes); (c) sensitive ecosystems/habitats. Slurry Faeces and urine mixed or not with some litter material and some water to give a liquid manure that flows under gravity and can be pumped. Slurry stores Fixed structures designed to hold slurry. Solid manure Faeces or droppings and urine mixed or not with litter material that do not flow under gravity and cannot be pumped. Waste water Rainwater run-off commonly mixed with manure, water derived from the cleaning of surfaces (e.g. floors) and equipment, and water derived from the operation of air cleaning systems. For the purposes of this document, effluents from other uses (e.g. black water / sewage, waste water from cleaning of on-farm storage of dead animals, water contaminated with fuels / oil) are not considered. Pollutants and parameters Term used Definition Total ammoniacal nitrogen Ammonium-N (NH 4 -N) and its compounds, including uric acid, which are readily broken down to NH 4 -N. Total nitrogen Total nitrogen, expressed as N, includes free ammonia and ammonium (NH 4 -N), nitrites (NO 2 -N), nitrates (NO 3 -N) and organic nitrogen compounds. Total nitrogen excreted Total nitrogen eliminated from animal metabolic processes through urine and faeces. Total phosphorus Total phosphorus, expressed as P 2 O 5 , includes all inorganic and organic phosphorus compounds, dissolved or bound to particles. Total phosphorus excreted Total phosphorus eliminated from animal metabolic processes through urine and faeces. Animal categories Term used Definition Breeders Parent poultry stock (males and females) kept for reproductive purposes. Broilers Chickens reared for meat production, not including breeders. Ducks Birds of the Anas and Cairina genus, reared for meat or egg production, not including breeders. Farrowing sows Female pig between the perinatal period and the weaning of the piglets. Fattening pigs Production pigs typically reared from a live weight of 30 kg to slaughter or first service. This category includes growers, finishers and gilts that have not been serviced. Gestating sows Pregnant sows, including gilts. Gilts Female pig after puberty and before farrowing. Mating sows Sows ready for service and before gestation. Heavy pigs Pigs with a live slaughter weight typically above 130 kg, due to extended production cycles. Pig An animal of the porcine species of any age, kept for breeding or fattening. Piglets Pigs from birth to weaning (typically until a live weight of around 8 kg). Poultry Fowl (chickens), turkeys, guinea fowl, ducks, geese, quails, pigeons, pheasants and partridges reared or kept in captivity for breeding, the production of meat or eggs for consumption, or for restocking supplies of game. Laying hens Grown female chickens when they start laying eggs after 16 to 20 weeks of age, not including breeders, nor pullets. Pullets When chickens are reared for breeding, pullets are young female and male chickens until 20 weeks of age. When chickens are reared for egg production, pullets are hens before they begin to lay eggs at 16 to 20 weeks of age. Sows Female pigs during the rearing periods of mating, gestating and farrowing. Turkeys Birds of the Meleagris genus reared for meat production, not including breeders. Weaners Young pigs reared from weaning until fattening, typically reared from a live weight of around 8 kg until a live weight of around 30 kg. GENERAL CONSIDERATIONS These Uniform Conditions for operating rules are consistent with the use of best available techniques for the activities listed in Annex Ia to the IED. This document also includes information on emerging techniques, in accordance with Article 70i of the IED. The best available techniques and emerging techniques in this document are neither prescriptive nor exhaustive. Other techniques may be used that ensure at least an equivalent level of environmental protection, including human health and climate protection. Information on the environmental performance of the techniques listed in Section 4 and Section 5 (e.g. efficiency for ammonia emissions reduction) is based on the information exchange pursuant to Article 70i of the IED, and on recognised international guidance (e.g. UNECE guidance document on ‘Options for ammonia mitigation’). Additional information on the environmental performance of the techniques or their combinations may be taken from Union, national or international sources (e.g. UNECE guidance documents or EMEP/EEA air pollutant emission inventory guidebook) that ensure the provision of data of an equivalent scientific quality. It is for Member States to validate, based on scientifically established methods: (a) the use of techniques not listed in this document that ensure at least an equivalent level of environmental protection, including human health and climate protection, also taking into account animal welfare systems recognised at Union, national or international level; (b) the use of environmental performance of techniques listed in this document that are different to those in Sections 4 and 5 or the environmental performance where not specified in Sections 4 and 5; (c) the environmental performance of combinations of techniques. All underlying data, required by Directive 2003/4/EC of the European Parliament and of the Council ( 8 ) , methodologies, and assumptions, must be fully documented and publicly available. 1.
