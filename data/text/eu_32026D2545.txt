DRAWING UP AND REVIEWING A BEST AVAILABLE TECHNIQUES (BAT) REFERENCE DOCUMENT 1.1 Context 1.1.1 Legislative context In 2024, Directive (EU) 2024/1785 ( 1 ) of the Parliament and the Council amended Directive 2010/75/EU ( 2 ) of the Parliament and the Council (‘the revised IED’), which lays down rules on integrated prevention and control of pollution arising from industrial activities. It also lays down rules designed to prevent or, where that is not practicable, to reduce continuously emissions into air, water and land, prevent the generation of waste, improve resource efficiency, and promote the circular economy and decarbonisation, in order to achieve a high level of protection of human health and the environment taken as a whole (see Article 1 of the revised IED). Article 13(1) of the revised IED, requires that the Commission organise an exchange of information between it and Member States, the industries concerned, non-governmental organisations promoting human health or environmental protection and the European Chemicals Agency in order to draw up, review and, where necessary, update BAT reference documents (‘BREFs’). The Commission should also ensure that the European Environment Agency participates in the exchange of information, where the exchange of information would benefit from the Agency's expertise ( 3 ) . Article 13(1) also sets out ‘that exchange of information shall aim at an eight-year review cycle of BAT reference documents prioritising the documents that have the highest potential to improve the protection of human health or the environment. The duration of the exchange of information … shall not exceed four years for each individual BAT reference document.’ The information exchange covers the steps between the kick-off meeting (KoM) and the final meeting of the technical working group (TWG). The preparatory activities for the information exchange (see Section 4.5) are expected to shorten this period to less than four years. Article 13(2) of the revised IED requires that ‘the exchange of information shall, in particular, address the following: (a) the performance of installations and techniques in terms of emissions, expressed as short- and long-term averages, where appropriate, and the associated reference conditions, consumption and nature of raw materials, water consumption, use of energy and generation of waste; (b) the techniques used, associated monitoring, cross-media effects, economic and technical viability and developments therein; (c) best available techniques and emerging techniques identified after considering the issues mentioned in points (a) and (b). ’ A BREF, resulting from this exchange of information, is defined in Article 3, point 11, of the revised IED. It is a document drawn up for defined activities describing, in particular, applied processes and techniques, present emission and consumption levels, techniques considered for the determination of BAT as well as BAT conclusions and any emerging techniques, giving special consideration to the criteria listed in Annex III to the revised IED ( 4 ) . Therefore, by definition, a BREF is a descriptive document and it does not prescribe the use of any technique or specific technology, nor does it interpret the revised IED. ‘Best available techniques (BAT) ’ are defined in Article 3, point 10, of the revised IED as the most effective and advanced stage in the development of activities and their methods of operation which indicates the practical suitability of particular techniques for providing the basis for emission limit values and other permit conditions designed to prevent and, where that is not practicable, to reduce emissions and the impact on the environment as a whole: a. ‘Techniques’ includes both the technology used and the way in which the installation is designed, built, maintained, operated and decommissioned. b. ‘Available techniques’ means techniques developed on a scale which allows implementation in the relevant industrial sector, under economically and technically viable conditions, taking into consideration the costs and advantages, regardless of whether such techniques are used or produced in the Union, as long as they are reasonably accessible to the operator. c. ‘Best’ means most effective in achieving a high general level of protection of the environment as a whole, including human health and climate protection. Article 3, point 12, of the revised IED also defines ‘BAT conclusions’ as the parts of a BREF laying down the conclusions on BAT and emerging techniques (ET), their description, information to assess their applicability, the emission levels associated with those techniques, the environmental performance levels associated with those techniques, the content of an environmental management system including benchmarks, associated monitoring, associated consumption levels and, where appropriate, relevant site remediation measures. The BAT conclusions are to be adopted through the procedure referred to in Article 75(2) of the revised IED. They shall be the reference for setting permit conditions for the installations covered by Chapter II of the IED (Article 14(3) of the revised IED). BAT conclusions should also identify ET and BAT that operators of industrial installations can implement to transform installations to be consistent with the Union’s objective of a sustainable, clean, circular and climate-neutral economy. ‘Emission levels associated with BAT’ are defined in Article 3, point 13, of the revised IED as the range of emission levels obtained under normal operating conditions using a BAT or a combination of BAT, as described in BAT conclusions, expressed as an average over a given period of time, under specified reference conditions. ‘Environmental performance levels associated with BAT’ are defined in Article 3, point 13a, of the revised IED as the range of environmental performance levels, obtained under normal operating conditions using a BAT or a combination of BAT, as described in BAT conclusions. ‘Environmental performance’ is defined in Article 3, point 13aa, of the revised IED as the performance with regard to consumption levels, resource efficiency concerning materials, water and energy resources, the reuse of materials and water, and waste generation. ‘Emerging techniques (ET)’ are defined in Article 3, point 14, of the revised IED as novel techniques for an industrial activity that, if commercially developed, could provide either a higher general level of protection of human health and the environment or at least the same level of protection of human health and the environment and higher cost savings than existing BAT. ‘ Emission levels associated with ET’ are defined in Article 3, point 48, of the revised IED as the range of emission levels obtained under normal operating conditions using an ET or a combination of ET, as described in BAT conclusions, expressed as an average over a given period of time, under specified reference conditions. ‘ Environmental performance levels associated with ET ’ are defined in Article 3, point 49, of the revised IED as the range of environmental performance levels, obtained under normal operating conditions, using an ET or a combination of ET as described in BAT conclusions. Benchmarks are defined in Article 3, point 13b, of the revised IED as the indicative range of environmental performance levels associated with BAT, which is to be used as a reference in the environmental management system. 1.1.2 Aim of a BREF The aim of a BREF is to determine BAT and ET, and to limit imbalances in the Union as regards the level of emissions from industrial activities. BREFs should provide information to the competent authorities of Member States, industrial operators, the Commission and the public at large on what BAT and ET are for the activities covered by Annex I to the revised IED. The process of determining BAT and ET should be transparent and objective, based on sound technical and economic information. A BREF is a driver towards improved protection of the environment and human health across the Union. To serve its main aim and ensure its user-friendliness, the content of the BREF should be limited to the relevant information for enabling the determination of BAT conclusions as set out in Chapter 3 ( 5 ) . A BREF is not meant to be a textbook on techniques to prevent and control pollution. Extensive literature exists on the subject to which the BREF can refer where needed. However, it is essential that the BREF provide information on the main techniques that were considered by the technical working group (TWG) (see Section 4.2 ( 6 ) ) for the determination of BAT and ET, and on the grounds for the BAT conclusions, together with key open issues or gaps in knowledge that need to be explored for the BREF’s subsequent review. 1.1.3 ‘Horizontal’ and ‘vertical’ BREFs BREFs are generally restricted to issues related to particular industrial activities (‘vertical’ BREFs). In some cases, BREFs may also address cross-sectoral issues that are common to all or many IED activities (‘horizontal’ BREFs). ‘Horizontal’ and ‘vertical’ BREFs should be developed so as to be complementary for the purpose of setting permit conditions for installations covered by the revised IED. ‘Vertical’ BREFs are to include sector-specific information. Consequently, the BAT conclusions mentioned in Article 21(3) of the revised IED, i.e. BAT conclusions relating to the main activity of an installation, are to be part of ‘vertical’ BREFs only. ‘Horizontal’ BREFs are to include information of a generic nature that can be used across many activities which fall under the scope of the revised IED. Information should be included in ‘horizontal’ BREFs where this supplements the information contained in ‘vertical’ BREFs on aspects that cut across several industrial sectors. This should not result in conflicting conclusions between ‘vertical’ and ‘horizontal’ BREFs. In order to facilitate the use of both ‘vertical’ and ‘horizontal’ BREFs in a complementary way, appropriate cross-references need to be made in a BREF to other relevant ‘vertical’ and ‘horizontal’ BREFs. 1.2 Procedure for the drawing up and reviewing of BREFs The Commission organises and coordinates the exchange of information and preparatory activities (early front-loading). The stakeholders involved in the exchange of information as provided for in Article 13(1) of the revised IED (Member States, the industries concerned, NGOs promoting human health or environmental protection, the European Chemicals Agency and the Commission) oversee the process through the Forum established in accordance with Article 13(3) of the revised IED. They contribute to the drawing up and reviewing of BREFs by participating in the technical working groups (TWGs). More information on the role of the stakeholders and the functioning of the Forum and the TWGs is given in Sections 1.3 and 4.2, respectively. The decision to draw up a BREF or to start reviewing a BREF is taken by the Commission and takes into account the opinion of the Forum on the work programme for the exchange of information, in accordance with Article 13(3), point (b), of the revised IED. 1.2.1 General procedure for the drawing up of a new BREF Since new activities are included in Annex I to the revised IED, new BREFs must be drawn up. In addition, new BREFs may also be drawn up for activities that are not already covered by existing BREFs or following a reorganisation of existing BREFs (e.g. merging existing BREFs into a new BREF). The workflow for the drawing up of a new BREF presents many similarities with the workflow for the review of a BREF and is described in Section 1.2.5. However, for a new BREF, special attention should be paid to the preparatory activities described in Section 4.5: a substantial amount of information may have to be gathered in the front-loading phase, which may take slightly longer than for a BREF review. 1.2.2 General procedure for the review of a BREF The reviewing of BREFs is a continuous process, due to the dynamic nature of BAT. Science and technologies are developing continuously, new techniques may emerge, new or emerging processes are being successfully introduced into the industry, new environmental concerns may arise and new knowledge on emitted substances and their hazardous properties may become available. In order to reflect such changes and their consequences for the BAT, BREFs have to be periodically reviewed and, if necessary, updated accordingly. This is explicitly addressed by Article 13(1) of the revised IED, which indicates that the exchange of information is to aim at an eight-year review cycle of BREFs. The decision to review a BREF should take into account: (i) information in the section of the BREF entitled ‘Concluding Remarks and Recommendations for Future Work’ (see Section 2.2); and (ii) information coming from INCITE (see Section 4.4), together with other factors such as: — an indication that new techniques might be available; — the need to expand the scope of the BREF; — the need to include products/substances or processes that were not yet covered; or — the need to explore particular aspects more thoroughly. 1.2.3 Partial review of a BREF When an existing BREF is reviewed as part of the programmed review cycle, it may be possible to review only a part of the BREF, focusing, for example, on a specific industrial process or subsector, on a specific environmental issue, or on a novel process that has emerged since the adoption of the BAT conclusions. Although this approach allows for a faster review, this may only be possible if the conditions below are met. — Some parts of the BREFs may remain unchanged, for example, if a subsector or production process has remained largely unchanged or if the performance of abatement techniques for a given pollutant observed in the field has not improved significantly. — The integrated approach of the IED is safeguarded. For example, if the partial review focuses on a specific environmental issue (e.g. a specific pollutant), it should not be limited to the BAT or associated emission or performance levels related to that issue. The review should also include possible cross-media effects (e.g. potential impact of the abatement techniques used to reduce this pollutant on water or energy consumption, waste generation, or potential emissions of other pollutants) and other environmental issues arising from the reduction or prevention of this specific environmental issue. — The overall consistency of the BAT conclusions is maintained. For example, if a BAT is developed for a novel process in a set of existing BAT conclusions, the conclusions regarding this new BAT must remain consistent with the conclusions on the BATs that are not part of the review (e.g. if the novel BAT is a generic BAT, it must be checked that it is applicable to all processes of the concerned industrial sector, and not only to this novel process). Carrying out a partial review of a BREF published before 2026 is not intended as the TWG would need to ensure that also the part not addressed in the partial review aligns (through a specific data collection exercise) with the objectives and requirements of the revised IED, in particular the chapter on BAT conclusions. The conclusions as to which parts of the BREF remain unchanged and which parts need to be reviewed are taken at the TWG’s kick-off meeting (see Section 4.6.1.2). 1.2.4 Objective of a BREF review The objective of a BREF review is to assess to what extent the previously adopted BAT conclusions are still fit for purpose, and, where necessary, to update these conclusions and identify evolutions in BAT and ET. This is achieved primarily by examining the parts of the BREF laying down the conclusions on BAT, by assessing how effective their implementation has been and by revising or updating those conclusions where new information is available that allows for doing so. The review of a BREF is, therefore, not expected to involve a complete rewrite or modification of the whole BREF. It is, however, recognised that in some cases the first reviews of BREFs could involve substantially more modifications than subsequent reviews. Clearly, the most relevant new information that needs to be identified, collected and submitted in the framework of the exchange of information for a BREF review is that which might lead to revised or updated BAT conclusions. For this purpose, it is crucial that data and information be collected on the basis of the guidance laid down in Chapter 5. Additionally, the review of a BREF should include: 1. improving, updating and complementing existing information and BAT conclusions on the basis of more recent data and information; 2. removing obsolete and outdated information; 3. correcting errors and removing any inconsistencies with other BREFs. The review of a BREF should mainly focus on the chapters of the BREF entitled ‘Techniques to Consider in the Determination of BAT and ET’, ‘Best Available Techniques (BAT) Conclusions’ and ‘Current emission and consumption levels’ and, to a lesser extent, on the chapters of the BREF entitled ‘Applied processes and techniques’ (in the event of important changes in this chapter). The review of the other chapters of the BREF should be given a lower priority. Overall, there is a need to ensure that all the information that remains within the BREF is as up-to-date, as consistent and as accurate as reasonably achievable. 1.2.5 Typical workflow for the drawing up and reviewing of BREFs The typical workflow for the drawing up and reviewing of BREFs includes as major milestones a kick-off meeting, a data and information collection phase, one draft of the BREF and a final meeting (see Section 4.6.1.4). The main steps for the drawing up and reviewing of BREFs are described in more detail in Table 1. These steps take into account: — the aim set out in Article 13(1) of the revised IED that a BREF is to be updated no later than eight years after the publication of the previous version; — the aim set out in Article 13(1) of the revised IED that ‘The duration of the exchange of information referred to in the first subparagraph shall not exceed four years for each individual BAT reference document’. The exact steps and timing of the work for each BREF depends on a number of factors, including the scope of the BREF (and the possible extension of the scope in the case of BREF reviews), the number and complexity of topics that need to be addressed, the resources of the European Bureau for Research on Industrial Transformation and Emissions - EU-BRITE - given its central role in the information exchange process and, above all, the level of active and effective participation of the TWG. The flexibility that exists to adapt the workflow to the specifics of a particular BREF applies without prejudice to the overall objective to complete the work within the time limits indicated above while ensuring the high quality of the BREF produced. It is expected that, in principle, two plenary TWG meetings should be held during the course of a BREF review (the kick-off meeting and the final meeting (see Section 4.6.1)). However, in certain cases (e.g. where a lot of new information has been provided, or when the determination of BAT is particularly controversial), interim TWG meetings (see Section 4.6.1.3) may be held. In addition to these plenary TWG meetings, data assessment workshops are organised (see Section 4.6.3.3) and subgroup meetings can be organised to facilitate the work (see Section 4.2.3). In order to make the most efficient use of resources from all those involved in the drawing up or reviewing of a BREF, there needs to be a clear deadline for submission of the bulk information promised or identified in the conclusions of the kick-off meeting (see Section 4.6.1.2). Information submitted after this deadline can be accepted only in exceptional circumstances, after consultation with the TWG, and can only be taken into account by EU-BRITE when it contributes substantially to deriving or updating conclusions on BAT or to the resolution of controversial or technically complicated issues. Should it be necessary, EU-BRITE will request additional information or clarifications from the TWG in order to collect any important missing information that is considered necessary, in particular for deriving BAT conclusions in accordance with the guidance laid down in Chapters 3 and 5. In the same way, EU-BRITE will inform TWG members of the next steps and possible deadlines in good time. 1.3 Forum established pursuant to Article 13 of the revised IED The Forum established under Article 13(3) of the revised IED is an expert group convened and chaired by the Commission and consists of representatives of Member States, the industries concerned and non-governmental organisations promoting the protection of human health or the environment. The appointment of members to the Forum is carried out in accordance with Commission Decision 2011/C 146/03 establishing the Forum ( 7 ) , which also sets out the task of the Forum. The Forum’s role, as explained in recital 14 and described in Article 13(3) of the revised IED, is to ensure an effective, active and transparent exchange of information resulting in high-quality BREFs by discussing and giving its opinion on the practical arrangements for the exchange of information. Specifically, in accordance with Article 13(3) of the revised IED, the Forum is to provide its opinion on the practical arrangements for the exchange of information, in particular, on: 1. the rules of procedure of the Forum; 2. the work programme for the exchange of information; 3. guidance on the collection of data; 4. guidance on the drawing up of BAT reference documents and on their quality assurance including the suitability of their content and format. Forum members are responsible for the nomination of their representatives to TWGs (see in particular Section 4.2.2 which addresses the tasks and profile of TWG members) and for keeping in contact with them throughout the drafting process to ensure an active and effective exchange of information. The Forum gives its opinion on the work programme for the exchange of information (i.e. the BREF work programme) at an early stage (e.g. two years in advance) to give stakeholders enough time to carry out preparatory work before the BREF review/drawing-up process starts, in particular the early front-loading (see Section 4.5.1). The Forum is also the place where general issues relating to the exchange of information are discussed. Through the Forum, stakeholders can express their opinions on the information exchange process, including lessons learned from the process and quality assurance aspects. The Forum may, if deemed necessary, suggest specific improvements or aspects that should be addressed during the drawing up or reviewing of a BREF. The main task of the Forum is to evaluate the outcome of the information exchange within TWGs, taking into account this guidance document, and to provide its opinion on the proposed content of the BREFs resulting from the work carried out at the technical level. To do so, following the finalisation of the work within the TWG, the updated final draft of a BREF will be sent to the Forum no later than eight weeks ahead of the Forum meeting where the document will be discussed ( 8 ) . Members of the Forum should submit all their comments on the draft final version of the BREF in writing in advance of the meeting. In accordance with Article 13(4) of the revised IED, ‘the opinion of the Forum on the proposed content of the BREFs ‘shall be submitted within six months of the final meeting of the technical working group’ (see Section 4.6.1.4). Considering the minimum period of eight weeks mentioned above, this means that the final draft of a BREF should be sent to the Forum no later than four months after the final meeting. The Forum may address, inter alia : 1. issues raised in the TWG for which the split view (see Section 4.6.1.4.2) of the TWG member concerned is not accurately reflected in the final draft text; 2. motivated proposals of Forum members to remove or modify a split view expressed by their own TWG representative; 3. clarification of text; 4. textual revisions that reflect more accurately the conclusion reached by the TWG; 5. corrections of typographic errors in the final draft; 6. proposals to bring important issues, which are already reported within the body of the BREF, to the ‘Concluding remarks and recommendations for future work’ section (see Section 2.3.9). Pursuant to Article 13(3) of the revised IED, the committee is involved in the discussion and adoption of the ‘guidance on the collection of data’ and of the ‘guidance on the drawing up of BAT reference documents and on their quality assurance including the suitability of their content and format’ [(i.e. this document)]. Article 13(5) of the revised IED stipulates that decisions on BAT conclusions are to be adopted in accordance with the examination procedure as laid down in Article 75(2) of the revised IED. Hence, the committee is to be involved in the adoption of decisions on BAT conclusions resulting from the exchange of information. After the adoption of the decision on the ‘BAT conclusions’, EU-BRITE will modify, if necessary, the BREF in accordance with the adopted decision on the BAT conclusions and will make the English version of the final BREF publicly available. The decisions on the BAT conclusions will be published in the Official Journal in the official languages of the Union.
