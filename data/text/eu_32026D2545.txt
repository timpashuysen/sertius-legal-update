L_202602545EN.000101.fmx.xml Official Journal of the European Union EN L series 2026/2545 20.11.2026 COMMISSION IMPLEMENTING DECISION (EU) 2026/2545 of 13 November 2026 amending Implementing Decision 2012/119/EU as regards the practical arrangements for the exchange of information referred to in Article 13(3), points (c) and (d), of Directive 2010/75/EU of the European Parliament and of the Council, the collection of data and the drawing up or review of BAT reference documents and their quality assurance (notified under document C(2026) 7990) (Text with EEA relevance) THE EUROPEAN COMMISSION, Having regard to the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, Having regard to Directive 2010/75/EU of the European Parliament and of the Council on industrial and livestock rearing emissions (integrated pollution prevention and control) ( 1 ) , and in particular Article 13 thereof, Whereas: (1) Commission Implementing Decision 2012/119/EU ( 2 ) lays down rules concerning guidance on the collection of data and on the drawing up of best available techniques (BAT) reference documents (BREFs) and on their quality assurance referred to in Article 13(3), points (c) and (d), of Directive 2010/75/EU. (2) Directive (EU) 2024/1785 of the European Parliament and of the Council ( 3 ) introduced substantial amendments to Directive 2010/75/EU. Those amendments provide, in particular, for: new environmental objectives; enhanced roles for the Commission — notably through the establishment of the Innovation Centre for Industrial Transformation and Emissions (INCITE) — as well as for the European Chemicals Agency (ECHA); a defined duration for the exchange of information and for the review cycle of BREFs; strengthened rules on the handling of confidential business information and commercially sensitive information; and new requirements for the permits that Member States grant to operators of industrial plants. (3) Pursuant to Article 14(3) of Directive 2010/75/EU, BAT conclusions set out in BREFs must be the reference for setting permit conditions. Therefore, in order to ensure coherence with the amendments of Directive (EU) 2024/1785, and in accordance with Article 13(1a) of Directive 2010/75/EU, the provisions of Implementing Decision 2012/119/EU must be updated by 1 July 2026. (4) In accordance with Article 13(3), point (c), of Directive 2010/75/EU, the Commission obtained the opinion of the forum on this Decision on 2 March 2026. (5) The measures provided for in this Decision are in accordance with the opinion of the Committee established by Article 75 of the Directive, HAS ADOPTED THIS DECISION: Article 1 The Annex to Implementing Decision 2012/119/EU is replaced by the Annex to this Decision. Article 2 This Decision is addressed to the Member States. Done at Brussels, 13 November 2026. Data/information submitted after the deadline may not be taken into account (see also Section 1.2.5). For emissions data, the TWG should conclude, on the basis of the concentration and (specific) load data gathered (if both data are available, they should both be submitted), if one or both types of data would contribute to deriving useful BAT conclusions. Specific loads (e.g. mass of pollutants released per mass of product manufactured) allow for the comparison of the environmental performance of installations irrespective of their different production volumes and are not influenced by mixing or dilution. Concentrations (accompanied by reference conditions and averaging periods) generally give more information on the short-term performance of individual processes or unit operations and thus can reveal fluctuations and peak emissions. They also establish the environmental performance at any given moment. Combining them with flow data allows the pollution load at any given moment to be established. When continuous measurements are used, concentrations can be used to provide information on performance over a longer time period (e.g. one year). 6 CHAPTER 6: QUALITY ASSURANCE OF THE DRAWING UP AND REVIEWING OF THE BREFS Ensuring the quality of the process of drawing up and reviewing of the BREFs largely rests on the adherence to the guidance contained in this document, in particular regarding the BREF content and boundaries and the collection of data for deriving BAT and ET. The quality of a BREF depends upon both the participants involved in the process (a high level of technical expertise and involvement is required) and the quality of the ‘Sevilla process’ itself. To guarantee this quality, Member States, industries concerned, non-governmental organisations promoting human health or environmental protection, the European Chemicals Agency and the Commission are each expected to have in place a quality system that includes: 1. clear definitions of responsibilities and allocation of tasks; 2. methods and procedures; 3. the allocation of sufficient resources (in particular staff); 4. an internal control system leading to continuous improvements. Ensuring the BREF is of good quality is a day-to-day activity which is based on the personal commitment of all those involved in the exchange of information. Generally, at the origin of the information collected, each individual TWG member has, as a first-level controller , a special role to play to guarantee the quality of his/her contributions. The EU-BRITE staff members who draft the BREFs based on contributions from the TWG are second-level controllers of the quality of the information submitted. The quality assurance process should be kept under constant review and the Forum will be asked to contribute to this.
