62025CJ0151_RES Case C‑151/25 [Viaudret] ( i ) F.B. v Région wallonne (Request for a preliminary ruling from the Conseil d’État) Judgment of the Court (Fifth Chamber) of 24 September 2026 (Reference for a preliminary ruling – Environment – Aarhus Convention – Article 6 – Public participation in decisions on specific activities – Extension of the duration of the validity of a permit to operate a wind farm – Obligation to organise a public participation phase prior to that extension) Environment – Assessment of the effects of certain projects on the environment – Directive 2011/92 – Project – Meaning – Renewal of an existing permit to operate an installation listed in Annex I or II – Not included (European Parliament and Council Directive 2011/92, Art. 1(2)(a)) (see paragraph 35) Environment – Integrated pollution prevention and control – Directive 2010/75 – Scope – Operation of a wind farm – Not included (European Parliament and Council Directive 2010/75, Art. 2(1) and (10) and Annex I) (see paragraph 36) Questions referred for a preliminary ruling – Jurisdiction of the Court of Justice – Interpretation of an international agreement concluded by the European Union and the Member States on the basis of shared competence – Convention on access to information, public participation in decision-making and access to justice in environmental matters (Aarhus Convention) – Included (Art. 267 TFEU; Aarhus Convention; Council Decision 2005/370) (see paragraph 38) International agreements – European Union Agreements – Convention on access to information, public participation in decision-making and access to justice in environmental matters (Aarhus Convention) – Provisions of that convention concerning public participation in decisions on specific activities – National legislation excluding from public participation any procedure to extend an authorisation granted for an activity covered by those provisions – Not permissible (Aarhus Convention, Art. 6; Council Decision 2005/370) (see paragraphs 39-55) Résumé Hearing a reference for a preliminary ruling from the Conseil d’État (Council of State, Belgium), the Court rules on the applicability of rules on public participation in decision-making laid down in Article 6 of the Aarhus Convention ( 1 ) to a procedure for the extension of an authorisation to operate a wind farm. Following a request to extend the duration of the validity of a permit to operate a wind farm in the Région wallonne (Walloon Region, Belgium) the competent national authority extended by 10 years the period of validity of that permit, which had initially been granted for a period of 20 years. In accordance with the applicable regional legislation, no public participation phase was organised prior to the adoption of that extension decision. A person residing close to that wind farm, of the view that such a decision could not be taken without the prior organisation of a public participation phase as provided for by the Aarhus Convention, brought an action before the Council of State for annulment of that extension decision. According to the Council of State, it is not disputed that the initial decision regarding the operation of the wind farm in question had to be adopted in compliance with the requirements relating to public participation laid down in Article 6 of the Aarhus Convention. However, that court is uncertain as to whether a decision extending by 10 years the authorisation to operate such a wind farm, initially granted for a duration of 20 years, must also be adopted in compliance with those requirements. The Council of State therefore decided to refer that question to the Court. Findings of the Court As the operation of the wind farm in question constitutes, in the present case, an activity listed in Annex I of the Aarhus Convention, the Court recalls that, under Article 6(1) of that convention, a decision authorising such an activity must, in principle, be preceded by a public participation phase in compliance with the requirements laid down in paragraphs 2 to 9 of that article. Article 6(10) of the Aarhus Convention also provides that the provisions of paragraphs 2 to 9 of that article apply ‘mutatis mutandis, and where appropriate’ where a public authority ‘reconsiders or updates the operating conditions for an activity referred to in paragraph 1 [of that article]’. Accordingly, a decision to extend the authorisation to operate a wind farm such as that in question may fall within the scope of the requirements relating to public participation laid down in paragraphs 2 to 9 of Article 6 of the Aarhus Convention if that decision entails a ‘reconsideration’ or ‘update’ of the ‘operating conditions for [that] activity’ within the meaning of paragraph 10 of that article. Observing that the concept of ‘operating conditions for an activity referred to in paragraph 1’ in Article 6(10) of the Aarhus Convention is not defined in that convention or the implementation guide, the Court carries out a literal, contextual and teleological interpretation of that provision. Regarding the literal interpretation of Article 6(10) of the Aarhus Convention, the Court notes that, in everyday language, the duration for which an activity is authorised constitutes one of the ‘operating conditions for [that] activity’. Consequently, any decision authorising the exercise of that activity beyond the period initially set is equivalent to an ‘update’ of the ‘operating conditions’ of that activity. The context of which that provision forms part and the objective pursued by the Aarhus Convention confirm that interpretation. As regards, more specifically, the objective pursued, the Court emphasises that it follows from Article 1 of the Aarhus Convention that Article 6 thereof is intended to contribute to protecting the right of every person of present and future generations to live in an environment adequate to his or her health and well-being. To that end, Article 6(7) of the Aarhus Convention, read in conjunction with paragraph 6 of that article, requires that the public be given the opportunity to submit any comments, information, analyses or opinions that it considers relevant having regard to the proposed activity. While it is true that, in the case of the renewal of the authorisation of an activity falling, as in the present case, under paragraph 20 of Annex I to the Aarhus Convention, the public had the opportunity to express its views at the time of the initial authorisation of that activity, it must be stated that, over time, both the technologies relevant for the activity concerned and the environment in which that activity is carried out are liable to have changed. Thus, on expiry of the initially granted authorisation, the question whether the activity concerned may be continued under the conditions provided for by that authorisation, whether those conditions must be updated, or whether the activity must, as the case may be, be discontinued, may arise. It would therefore run counter to the objective pursued by Article 6 of the Aarhus Convention to allow a decision to renew an authorisation such as that in the main proceedings, which consists of extending by 10 years an authorisation initially granted for a period of 20 years, to be adopted in all cases without ensuring public participation. Consequently, where, as in the present case, the applicable national legislation excludes the adoption of such a decision from public participation, it is for the national court to assess whether it is appropriate to apply the provisions of Article 6(2) to (9) of the Aarhus Convention mutatis mutandis, taking into account the specific circumstances of each case. In the light of the foregoing, the Court holds that Article 6(1) and (10) of the Aarhus Convention must be interpreted as precluding national legislation under which any procedure for a decision to extend by 10 years an authorisation for an activity listed in Annex I to that convention, initially granted for a period of 20 years, is excluded from public participation.
