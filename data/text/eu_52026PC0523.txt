CONTEXT OF THE PROPOSAL • Reasons for and objectives of the proposal Hydrogen is an integral part of the European Union's strategy for achieving climate neutrality by 2050, as outlined in the EU Hydrogen Strategy 1 . The strategy positions hydrogen as a versatile energy carrier capable of decarbonising sectors that are challenging to electrify. By promoting the production and use of renewable and clean hydrogen, the EU aims to scale hydrogen technologies, reduce dependency on fossil fuels, and propel sustainable economic growth. This vision is further supported by the REPowerEU plan 2 , which aims to reduce dependency on fossil fuel imports and accelerate the transition to renewable energy sources, including clean hydrogen production and utilization across various sectors. The potential of hydrogen to facilitate decarbonisation is acknowledged within the UNECE framework, where it is considered a viable fuel option for non-road mobile machinery (NRMM) 3 . This recognition highlights hydrogen’s environmental benefits and its capacity to significantly lower emissions from NRMM engines. Despite this international acknowledgment, hydrogen is not listed as a reference fuel under Article 25 of Regulation (EU) 2016/1628. The absence of hydrogen in the regulatory framework limits the NRMM sector's ability to transition to more sustainable technologies, hindering the EU's goals of reducing carbon emissions and dependence on fossil fuels as outlined in the European Green Deal 4 . Title of the proposal/initiative REGULATION OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL amending Regulation (EU) 2016/1628 as regards the inclusion of hydrogen as a reference fuel for the testing of an engine type or engine family 1.2. Policy area(s) concerned The amendment to include hydrogen as a reference fuel for non-road mobile machinery (NRMM) in Regulation (EU) 2016/1628 primarily concerns several key policy areas: Environmental and Climate Policy : The proposal aligns with the EU’s broader environmental goals, particularly in terms of reducing emissions and mitigating climate change impacts. It supports initiatives like the European Green Deal, aiming to transform the EU into a sustainable economy with minimal net emissions of greenhouse gases. Energy Policy : The inclusion of hydrogen fuel reflects the EU's strategic direction in diversifying energy sources and promoting renewable and clean energy technologies. This is consistent with the EU Hydrogen Strategy, which seeks to leverage hydrogen's potential as a versatile energy carrier. Industrial Policy : By fostering the adoption of hydrogen technology, the proposal supports innovation and competitiveness within the EU's NRMM industrial sector. It encourages research and development, leading to technological advancements and the creation of new market opportunities in the NRMM sector. Single Market Policy : The harmonization of standards across Member States is crucial for maintaining the integrity of the EU’s internal market. The proposal facilitates the free movement of goods, reduces technical trade barriers, and ensures a level playing field for manufacturers and economic operators. Health Policy : By reducing emissions of pollutants, such as nitrogen oxides and particulate matter, the proposal contributes to better air quality and public health outcomes, aligning with EU objectives to improve the well-being of its citizens. These policy areas collectively support the EU’s strategic objectives of sustainability, competitiveness, and market harmonization, reflecting an integrated approach to addressing environmental and economic challenges. 1.3. Objective(s) 1.3.1. General objective(s) The general objective of amending Regulation (EU) 2016/1628 to include hydrogen as a reference fuel for internal combustion engines used in non-road mobile machinery (NRMM) is to close the existing regulatory gap between the current EU regulations and the international standards set by the UNECE, which already recognise hydrogen as a possible fuel option. This amendment promotes technological advancement and environmental sustainability within the NRMM sector by facilitating the transition to cleaner energy solutions. By addressing this regulatory alignment, the initiative ensures cohesion and consistency in the Union's approach to climate change, innovation, and market harmonization, thereby supporting competitive advantage for EU manufacturers in global markets. 1.3.2. Specific objective(s) Specific objective No. 1 : Facilitate decarbonization, environmental sustainability, and enhancing energy security Hydrogen is a recognised fuel in the EU’s strategy to reduce carbon emissions and achieve climate neutrality by 2050. By including hydrogen into the regulatory framework for NRMM, this initiative contributes directly to the decarbonisation of a traditionally fossil-fuel-dependent sector. Diversifying energy sources by including hydrogen helps reduce the EU's dependence on fossil fuels. Through initiatives like the EU Hydrogen Strategy, this move supports the development of a sustainable and secure energy system, contributing to the overall resilience of the EU's energy supply. Specific objective 2: Stimulating Innovation and Industrial Competitiveness By recognising hydrogen as a reference fuel, the amendment encourages innovation within the NRMM sector, stimulating research and development of new technologies. This initiative supports the objectives of the EU industrial strategy, which seeks to harness and advance new technological solutions. The initiative ensures that EU regulations are harmonised with UNECE Regulation No. 96 Revision 04, fostering an integrated approach to NRMM and motor vehicle emissions regulations. This alignment is essential for facilitating international trade and maintaining the competitiveness of EU manufacturers on the global stage. 1.3.3. Expected result(s) and impact Specify the effects which the proposal/initiative should have on the beneficiaries/groups targeted. The proposal is expected to deliver significant benefits across multiple dimensions, supporting the EU’s goals for climate neutrality, market integration, and global leadership in clean technology. Specific impact 1: Environmental Impact The use of hydrogen helps lower emissions of greenhouse gases and pollutants, contributing to improved air quality and supporting the EU’s climate neutrality goals. The initiative supports the transition to cleaner energy solutions, fostering a long-term shift towards sustainable industrial practices in the NRMM sector. Specific impact 2: Economic Impact EU manufacturers and suppliers along the NRMM value chain are expected to benefit from access to a harmonised regulatory framework that aligns with international standards, enhancing competitiveness in both domestic and global markets. The regulation fosters technological innovation by promoting research and development of hydrogen technologies, potentially leading to new business opportunities, jobs, and growth within the green economy. Specific impact 3: Market and Trade Impact The initiative ensures that NRMM products can be seamlessly traded across EU Member States, reducing technical barriers and fostering a more integrated internal market. Alignment with international standards facilitates smoother access to global markets, strengthening the EU's export potential. Specific impact 4: Regulatory Impact The amendment ensures consistent application of environmental regulations across Member States, reducing disparities and simplifying compliance processes for businesses. It aligns with and reinforces broader EU policies, such as the European Green Deal and Hydrogen Strategy, by embedding these objectives into regulatory practice. Specific impact 5: Stakeholder Impact Manufacturers and suppliers will benefit from clear regulatory environment and harmonised NRMM emission regulations, which will enhance their competitiveness. Citizens will experience indirect benefits through improved air quality and public health, as reduced emissions from NRMM contribute to a cleaner and healthier environment. 1.3.4.
